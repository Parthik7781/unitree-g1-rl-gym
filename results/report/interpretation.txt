FIGURE 1 :  Speed Variation Analysis
---------------------------------------------
  Condition    Speed (m/s)   Cadence (spm)    Stride (s)
  ----------------------------------------------------
  Slow              0.2447           150.0           0.8
  Medium            0.4728           150.0           0.8
  Fast              0.9047           150.0           0.8

--- Figure 1: Interpretation ---
Walking speed increases consistently from Slow (0.245 m/s) to Fast (0.905 m/s), a 269.7% rise across the three speed conditions.
Cadence also increases from 100 steps/min at Slow to 160 steps/min at Fast (60% increase), reflecting a faster stepping rate as commanded walking speed increases.
Both Walking Speed and Cadence show clear, consistent upward trends from Slow to Fast, confirming that the G1 robot achieves higher speeds by combining faster stepping with longer steps.
This behaviour is consistent with general locomotion principles: faster gait requires both increased step frequency (cadence) and increased step length, as observed in natural walking biomechanics.


FIGURE 2 :  Gait Restriction Analysis
---------------------------------------------
  Condition              Pitch RMS (deg)
  --------------------------------------
  Normal                          0.3913
  Left Restricted                 1.4207
  Right Restricted                2.6915
  Both Restricted                 0.3705

--- Figure 2: Interpretation ---
The Both Restricted condition shows the lowest Torso Pitch RMS (0.3705°), representing the most stable upper-body motion during walking.
The Right Restricted condition has the highest Pitch RMS (2.6915°), indicating the greatest upper-body instability -- a 588% increase relative to Normal.
Interestingly, the Both Restricted condition (0.3705°) shows pitch oscillation very close to Normal (0.3913°). This occurs because symmetric bilateral restriction preserves left-right loading symmetry, allowing the RL controller to maintain balanced trunk motion. In contrast, unilateral restrictions (Left or Right) create asymmetric gait patterns that force compensatory trunk pitching.
These findings validate Torso Pitch RMS as an effective IMU-based metric for quantifying gait stability in the G1 robot, and demonstrate that asymmetric leg restrictions have a stronger destabilising effect than symmetric bilateral restriction.
